## Post Text (exact copy to paste)

```
Senior engineers are describing something new with AI tools.

"I feel like a middleman in my own work."

Not "I'm scared of AI." Not "I don't deserve this."

This is different: you did the work, you earned the title — but the work happened to you rather than through you. The AI generated it. You reviewed and approved it. You shipped it.

That's not imposter syndrome. Imposter syndrome is a confidence problem. This is an ownership problem. They look similar. They need different fixes.

The craft feeling used to come from the struggle — the hours stuck on a hard problem, the breakthrough that came from wrestling with it yourself. The struggle was the training. AI removes the struggle. That's the point. But it also removes the training. And the ownership.

The people navigating this well aren't rejecting AI. They're doing something more practical: staying calibrated.

Three things that help:
→ One no-AI session per week. Real work, without AI. Just to stay honest with yourself.
→ The Explanation Requirement: before you ship anything AI helped build, explain it out loud to no one. Why that approach? What are the tradeoffs? If you can't explain it, you don't own it yet.
→ One honest measurement: "What can I still do without AI?" Track the answer over time.

The engineers who figure this out early will use AI as a lever while maintaining ownership of the work.

The question isn't "should I use AI?" It's "what do I want to still be able to do yourself in two years?"

That's worth answering before you lose the answer.

Have you noticed this shift in your own work?
```
