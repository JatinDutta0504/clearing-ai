The Compounding Deficit

May 28, 2026 · 8 min read · For software engineers navigating AI fatigue

Tonight's Thought

Albert Bartlett, a physics professor at the University of Colorado, famously said: "The greatest shortcoming of the human race is our inability to understand the exponential function."

He was talking about population and resource limits. But replace those words with "skill decay from AI tooling," and the statement lands with uncomfortable precision.

Two percent. That is approximately how much less you learn per coding session when AI writes the code instead of you.

Small enough to ignore. Compounding enough to matter.

The Math Nobody Talks About

If you write a feature yourself, you spend 90 minutes and retain roughly 60% of what you learned. You can explain it months later. It integrates with your existing model of how systems work.

If AI writes the same feature in 15 minutes, you spend 10 minutes reviewing it and retain roughly 20% of what you would have if you had written it yourself. You can use it. You cannot explain it. It does not integrate with anything — it is an isolated fact with no roots.

The Compounding Problem

The first session: 40 percentage points of learning difference. One session. Insignificant.

Session 100: You have done 100 features with AI. You have retained the output of 100 features without building the underlying knowledge. Your skill model has 100 entries — but no foundation.

Session 500: You can produce. You cannot pattern-match. Your skill model is 500 features wide and 3 neurons deep.

What This Looks Like in the Wild

You are a fourth-year engineer. You can build anything. You can explain nothing. You pass reviews, ship features, get decent performance ratings.

And then in your fifth year, someone asks you to design a system from scratch — no AI, no reference, just you and a blank screen — and you realize you have never actually built anything without help. You were producing. You were not learning.

This is not a morale problem. It is an economic problem. And it compounds quietly, the same way compound interest works in reverse: against you, slowly at first, then all at once.

Who Stops Compounding

2% estimated daily learning deficit per AI-assisted session vs. solo writing
4th–5th yr career when the compounding deficit becomes visible and undeniable

The experience trap: the highest-risk engineers are not juniors. Juniors know they are learning. They do not assume they understand everything. They ask questions and retain more because they are actively paying attention.

Mid-career engineers are the most vulnerable. They have enough experience to feel confident that they understand how things work. They do not realize that experience was built pre-AI — and that each AI-assisted sprint is quietly subtracting from the foundation that experience represents.

The One Thing That Undoes It

Before AI this week: open a document. Write in your own words what the system you are building does.

Not what the docs say. What you understand it to do.

What you cannot write with confidence: that is the 2% worth fighting for.

Weekly Reflection

Your next task: before you reach for AI, ask — what will I retain from this session? If the answer is "not much," and the feature is doable without AI: do it without AI. That is the 2% recovery. One session at a time.

The Framework Worth Using

Three-part Decision Protocol

  1. Daily question: What will I retain from this session? If not much — and the feature is doable without AI — do it without AI.
  2. Weekly truth check: Can I explain the last 3 features I shipped without a reference? Not the PR description. My own words, two days later.
  3. The cost question: Will not knowing this matter in three months? If yes: retain the learning. If no: use the tool. But make that call consciously.

What Stops the Compounding

A week of no AI and you start noticing: the gaps are smaller than you thought. Two weeks and the scaffolding starts to rebuild. Four weeks and the confidence gap is visibly closing.

Stop. Rest. Deliberate practice. The deficit stops compounding when you stop feeding it.
