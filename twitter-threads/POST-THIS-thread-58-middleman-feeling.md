**Tweet 1:**
Software engineers are increasingly describing their experience with a phrase that never came up in tech 5 years ago:

"I feel like a middleman in my own work."

Let me explain what they mean — and why it matters. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2:**
You built it. You shipped it. But you didn't really build it.

AI generated the code. AI suggested the approach. AI caught the edge cases. You reviewed it, approved it, integrated it, shipped it.

The work happened. The learning didn't.

You became a ratification layer instead of a creation layer. That's the middleman feeling.

---

**Tweet 3:**
Here's the thing nobody says clearly enough:

The middleman feeling isn't imposter syndrome. Imposter syndrome is "I don't deserve this." What engineers describe is different: "I deserve this, I did the work, but the work happened to me rather than through me."

One is a confidence problem. The other is an ownership problem. They sound similar. They require different fixes.

---

**Tweet 4:**
The craft feeling used to come from the struggle.

You sat with a problem for hours. You got stuck, unstuck, stuck again. You eventually solved it and the solving had shaped you — you understood the solution at a structural level. The struggle was the training.

AI removes the struggle. That's the point. But it also removes the training. And the feeling of "this is mine."

---

**Tweet 5:**
Why this is hard to talk about:
- You look productive. Metrics are fine. You're shipping.
- You're "supposed to" be grateful for the tools.
- When you say "I feel like a middleman," people hear "I'm scared of AI."
- The industry keeps framing this as adaptation rather than loss.

You're not scared. You're grieving something real.

---

**Tweet 6:**
What helps:

1. Regular no-AI sessions — work on something real without AI, just to stay in touch with what you can still do
2. The Explanation Requirement — before you ship AI-generated code, explain it out loud to no one. If you can't explain the mechanism, you don't own it yet
3. Track your capability unaided — one honest measurement of "what can I still do without AI?"

---

**Tweet 7:**
The engineers who figure this out early will do something important: they'll use AI as a tool while maintaining ownership of the work.

Not by rejecting AI. By staying calibrated.

The question isn't "should I use AI?" It's "what do I want to still be able to do myself in two years?"

That's the question worth answering before you lose the answer.
