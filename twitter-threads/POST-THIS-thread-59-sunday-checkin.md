---

**Tweet 1:**
It's Sunday night.

Tomorrow you open your laptop and AI has already solved half the problems you'll face today.

You close tickets. You review PRs. You ship features that were mostly built by something else.

And you go home not really sure what you learned this week.

That feeling has a name. Most people don't talk about it. Let's talk about it. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2:**
Here's a question worth asking every Sunday night:

"What did I figure out this week that I couldn't have figured out a month ago?"

Not "what did I ship." Not "what did AI help me build." What did you — specifically, uniquely, with your brain — figure out?

If that question makes you pause, that's the Sunday night check-in gap.

---

**Tweet 3:**
Most engineers don't do this check-in. Here's why:

1. The velocity metrics look fine — you're shipping more than ever
2. AI makes it feel like you're learning — but processing isn't the same as learning
3. The industry has trained you to measure output, not growth
4. There's no vocabulary for "I'm working hard but not getting better at the things that matter"

Velocity ≠ growth. Shipping more ≠ learning more.

---

**Tweet 4:**
The research on this is uncomfortable:

Gloria Mark at UC Irvine found that after interruption, it takes an average of 23 minutes to fully regain focus on a task.

AI tools create a specific kind of interruption — one that feels like productivity. Your brain registers AI-generated success as your success. You never lose focus. You also never deep dive.

The cost is invisible until it compounds.

---

**Tweet 5:**
What the engineers who stay sharp have in common: a weekly calibration habit.

Not complicated. Just honest:

One hour Sunday night (or Monday morning). No AI. Work on something you actually care about — something real. No toy problems. Something you actually want to understand better.

Then ask yourself: what did I actually learn?

---

**Tweet 6:**
The second part of the habit: write it down.

Not a todo list. Not a task log. A one-paragraph answer to the question:

"What do I now understand that I didn't understand a week ago?"

That's the feedback loop AI tools accidentally delete.

The goal isn't to reject AI. The goal is to make sure you're still in the loop.

---

**Tweet 7:**
The engineers who navigate this well aren't the ones who use AI the least.

They're the ones who stay honest with themselves about what they can still do without it.

Sunday night check-in. One hour. One honest paragraph.

Start there.
